# 2-Minute Pitch

RAPIS, the Rwanda Agricultural Productivity Intelligence System, addresses a practical challenge in Track 1 of the NISR Big Data Hackathon: understanding differences in agricultural productivity and turning data into useful planning intelligence.

Our prototype uses NISR Seasonal Agricultural Survey Season B 2026 tables to analyze crop yields, production, irrigation, fertilizer use, erosion protection and agroforestry across Rwanda's districts.

Instead of only reporting statistics, RAPIS transforms the tables into an interactive analytical system. Users can explore crop productivity, compare districts and examine how agricultural practice indicators relate to reported yields.

Our next-stage system will combine approved NISR microdata with rainfall, temperature, soil, elevation and satellite-derived indicators. We will then validate predictive models and provide a Rwanda map showing evidence-based productivity patterns and decision-support information.

The target users are agricultural planners, government institutions, development partners, researchers and civil society organizations.

The key principle is simple: turn Rwanda's agricultural data into actionable evidence while clearly separating observed associations from predictions and causal claims.
